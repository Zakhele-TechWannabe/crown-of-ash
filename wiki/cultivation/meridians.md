# Meridians and circulation

The human network has **16 nodes and 24 directed channels**. The lower dantian is active; future middle and upper centres are present but closed. Breath connects through abdominal and lumbar alternatives, with routes to both palms and soles and returns toward the torso.

Width limits capacity; conductivity affects movement under pressure. Damage and strain can make a nominally good route unsafe. Tempering grades contribute to channels using regional weights, so a route that suits one body may be poor for another.

| Operation | Role in practice |
|---|---|
| Absorb | Admit ambient qi within the available supply and bodily route limits |
| Guide | Move qi through a directed route under its pressure conditions |
| Split | Coordinate multiple outgoing lanes; higher control demand |
| Merge | Coordination instruction; does not itself transfer qi in the current reference model |
| Filter | Improve admitted qi quality at a processing cost |
| Stabilise | Reduce instability at a processing cost |
| Integrate | Commit funded qi to bodily development |
| Ground | Discharge qi through a grounding route |
| Hold | Keep a timed pause in the programme |
| Release | Execute a bounded, paid external delivery assay |
| Circulate | Pump directed lanes using head from the opened dantian |

**Circulate unlocks after retention (layer 1)**. Its driving head is **2.8 × dantian pressure**. It can withdraw stored reserve into mobile flow; returned qi retains that reserve's quality. Pushing against adverse pressure adds channel strain. It does not create qi.

**Still Basin** is the ordinary starting programme; **Returning River** is the Palace starting programme. Both switch to opened schedules after retention. A typical practice session lasts **4 hours**, but actual core flow depends on pressure, route and instruction duty.

The layer-7 weekly drill uses guided circulation on both dantian return lanes. A passive Guide previously left one lane stalled against the filling vessel's back-pressure.
