The Long Road

The game starts with twenty servants in a village under a mountain and no sect at all. Over a hundred or a thousand years they will cultivate, take disciples, make enemies, form families and factions, and found the sect themselves. The machine plays every life. You watch, and set the rules. This page lays out every system that makes that happen, so we can work on them together before building.

The premise

Nothing is given. Everything is earned.

No roster, no elders, no ranks at the start. Every position in the sect exists only once someone has met its conditions, and is held only by someone who qualifies. The first sect master is a villager's grandchild, or their great-great-grandchild. The chronicle records the whole story.

What you do

You are the heavens, not a character

  • Watch at any speed, from 1x up to "skip a century".
  • Set rules and policies later: zones, priorities, sect laws.
  • Get pinged on big moments: an awakening, a founding, a schism, a breakthrough.
  • Inhabit nobody. Follow anybody.
A likely course · nothing here is scripted

From hamlet to sect

Year 0the hamlet

Twenty mortals

Farming, crafts, families. The only paths open are skills (up to level 35) and Body Tempering, the mortal martial road that hard labour and practice slowly open. No one can sense qi yet.

Years 5–80awakening

The first spirit root is revealed

A root is hidden until something reveals it:

  • a child born with one (about 3% of births)
  • an insight while meditating at the spirit spring
  • a spirit fruit found in the dark forest
  • a wandering cultivator on the West Road who tests the village youth
  • a broken manual dug out of old ruins

With no manual the awakened one gropes blindly at 10% speed.

+10–40 yrsfirst cultivator

Qi Condensation, layer 1

The first immortal of the valley. Their lifespan rises to 120. Villagers call them xianshi (immortal master), and their opinion of the cultivator jumps. A cult of reverence, or of envy, starts here.

+5–30 yrsa lineage

The first master and disciple

The cultivator accepts a rooted youth. Teaching gives the disciple +50% speed and passes on the technique. A lineage now exists, with its own name, seniority and loyalty.

+20–100 yrsa hall

The Hall faction forms

It needs three or more cultivators in one lineage, a claimed qi site (the peak) and a named founder. It starts taking a share of the harvest, and its first internal posts open.

+50–300 yrsfounding

A sect is founded

It needs all of these:

  • the founder at Foundation Establishment
  • a complete technique
  • at least five cultivators
  • a qi site of grade 2 or higher
  • stores for a year

The founder names it, and the chronicle writes the founding day. Positions unlock as the sect grows (table below).

Centuriesgrowth

Ranks, peaks, rivals, schisms

Recruitment fairs, outer and inner courts, elders, then several peaks. Internal factions and rival sects follow. A bitter elder may leave with followers and found a rival sect across the valley. None of it is scripted, and all of it can happen.

Three roads

How a mortal climbs

  • The qi road needs a spirit root. It is fast, long-lived and leads all the way up.
  • The body road is open to anyone. It is slow, painful and needs resources, and it plateaus near Foundation strength. It suits the rootless and martial villagers.
  • The craft road is skills to level 35. Beyond that a craftsman needs qi, their own or a patron's spirit resources.
Hidden until revealed

Spirit roots

Ten root types: the five phases (Metal, Wood, Water, Fire, Earth) and five mutations (Wind, Thunder, Ice, Light, Shadow). A person has 1–5 of them, and a purity from 0 to 100.

The ladder

Realms

Each layer has a qi cost and a bottleneck. Realm boundaries are major breakthroughs that can fail. A realm also lifts the skill ceiling: 35 for mortals, 40 at Qi Condensation, 45 at Foundation, 50 at Core Formation.

Breakthroughs

Every boundary is a gamble

chance = base + pill + insight + seclusion + willpower/2 − heart demons base: layer 80% · Foundation 30% · Core 12% · Nascent Soul 5% cap 95%
  • Heart demons come from the relationship system. Unresolved grudges, guilt, a betrayed master and a dead spouse all add risk. A person at peace breaks through more easily.
  • Failure means losing 10–40% of the stored qi, an injury that takes weeks to years to heal, and occasionally a damaged foundation that permanently slows them. Death is off by default. You can switch it on.
  • Being stuck slowly raises the chance (+1% per year at the bottleneck) and adds frustration to mood.
What makes a cultivator different

Physiques, techniques, resources

  • Physiques are about 1 in 5,000: Stone body, Cold-yin body, Pure-yang body, Spirit-sensitive eyes, Hundred meridians. Each changes growth, roads or breakthroughs.
  • Techniques have a grade, an element and a realm cap. A Yellow technique stops at Qi Condensation 9. They come from ruins, wanderers, trade, or creation: someone at Foundation or above with high comprehension can write a new technique in an insight. That is how a sect's own lineage art is born.
  • Qi density belongs to places. The peak, the spring and later formations all raise it, so who lives where is political.
  • Resources: spirit grains already exist. Pills, spirit stones, spirit herbs and beast cores arrive with alchemy and the dark forest.
  • Lifespan rises with each realm (70 mortal, 120, 250, 500, 1,000). Running out of years before a breakthrough is a real fear.

Earning XPXP/hour = base × key attribute/10 × comprehension × age × mood × teacher × difficulty × tools × traits × modifiers
  • 40% spills over to the discipline, and 5% to related disciplines.
  • Easy work gives 10% XP once you are 5 or more levels above it, so you plateau on chores.
  • A teacher 5 or more levels higher adds +50%, but can only lift you to their own level − 1.
  • First-time work, failures (50%) and children watching their parents (20%) all count.
Gates and insights
  • Gates sit at levels 5, 10 … 45. Each discipline sets its own conditions: days practised, a full season cycle, a milestone work, a master teacher, an insight.
  • Insights are a daily roll while stuck, scaled by comprehension, willpower and luck. Storms, festivals and grief can trigger one.
  • Rust: above level 15, unused skills fade slowly, but never below their tier floor.
  • Modifiers such as spirit fruits, tea, manuals, formations and qi-rich ground all feed one general system.
The model

Every tie is a small record

A relationship only exists once two people have reason to know each other. Each side keeps its own view, because A may adore B while B barely notices A.

opinion−100…100how much A likes B
familiarity0…100how well A knows B; grows with proximity and shared work, fades with absence
trust0…100separate from liking. You can respect a rival you trust, or love a liar.
bondsflagsfriend, sworn sibling, romance, master, disciple, rival, enemy, benefactor, debtor…
memoriesup to 3the strongest events between them, each with a weight that decays: "saved my son from the river (+60)".

Friend

Opinion ≥ 40 and familiarity ≥ 30. Friends share food when hungry, teach each other, and back each other in disputes.

Sworn sibling

Friends at ≥ 80 who go through a trial together (a famine, a beast attack) may swear an oath. It is permanent and binds like kin, and betraying it is a huge heart demon.

Benefactor / debt of grace

A saved life or a great kindness creates a debt the debtor feels bound to repay, sometimes generations later, by a grandchild.

Rival

Two people competing for the same thing (a post, a master, a spouse, a breakthrough resource) with opinion < 10. Rivals push each other's XP +10% and resent each other's wins.

Enemy and grudge

Opinion ≤ −60 with a cause. A grudge has an intensity, a cause, and an inheritable flag. It ends through an apology with compensation, mediation, a duel, time, or revenge. Revenge is humiliation, injury or theft by default, not death.

Romance and Dao companion

Mutual opinion ≥ 50, age and status fit. It leads to courtship, a marriage arranged with the families' approval, and children. Between cultivators it can become a Dao companionship: shared cultivation, +10% to both, and a deep heart demon if lost.

Blood

Family, households and clans

  • Kin is computed, never stored. Each person keeps only their mother and father ids. Siblings, cousins, grandparents and in-laws are walked from those links when needed.
  • Households share a home, food and work. They split when a son marries and builds, and merge when elders move in.
  • Clans are the descendants of a founding ancestor sharing a surname. They form once a lineage reaches 3 generations and 10 or more living members. They have a head, a reputation, and an ancestral hall later.
  • Marriage needs mutual opinion, the parents' approval (their opinion of the match and the other family's standing) and a matchmaker. Cultivators prefer cultivators. A rooted child raises a family's status sharply.
  • Children inherit attributes, Potential and roots from their parents with some noise. A root is far likelier when a parent has one, so cultivator dynasties emerge.
  • Duty: filial piety means caring for elders, mourning (a year's reduced work) and obeying the clan head. Neglect hurts reputation.
  • Inheritance: houses, land plots, tools, manuals and treasures. Disputes over inheritance are a classic source of grudges.
Dao lineage

Masters and disciples

  • Acceptance needs the master to be at least 5 levels or one realm above, opinion ≥ 20, an aptitude test, and a free slot. A master holds 1 personal disciple at Qi Condensation, 3 at Foundation and 6 at Core. Crafts use the same bond: a master smith takes an apprentice.
  • The master's duties: teaching (+50% XP, technique transmission), resources, protection, and finding the disciple a place.
  • The disciple's duties: obedience, labour, filial piety toward the master, and defending or avenging the master's name.
  • Seniority is computed by entry date within a lineage. It gives the forms of address: senior brother, junior sister, martial uncle, grand-master. Seniority matters for promotions and disputes.
  • Betrayal (defecting, stealing a technique, harming the master) is the biggest event in the system. It creates a grudge across the whole lineage, the Law Hall's first case, and sometimes a schism.
  • Legacy: when a master dies or vanishes into seclusion, their notes, technique and treasures pass to the chosen heir. Choosing the heir creates rivals.

Groups with a will

Factions

A faction is any group the sim treats as an actor. It has a leader, members, goals, money and relations with other factions. Factions emerge from rules and are never placed by hand.

Each faction tracks
  • Leader and succession rule: designation, eldest, strongest, election, or contest.
  • Members: each person stores their factions. Loyalty is opinion of the leader + benefits received + kin inside + doctrine fit.
  • Cohesion is the average loyalty minus the cliques' grievances.
  • Reputation covers righteous, demonic or neutral standing, plus fame.
  • Wealth is stores, sites, manuals and treasures.
  • Doctrine mixes martial, scholarly, mercantile, reclusive and expansionist, which shapes its choices.
  • Goals are things like "found a sect", "take the spirit spring" or "avenge the founder".
  • Relations is an opinion matrix between factions, plus treaties, tributes and feuds.
Dynamics
  • Schism: cohesion < 30 plus a rival leader with followers makes the faction split. Half the sects in the world are born this way.
  • Absorption: a weak faction joins a strong one, by choice or by pressure.
  • Politics: peaks compete for disciples, resources and the next Sect Master seat.
  • Conflict (no death by default): disputes, sabotage, duels, theft, sieges of trade. Wars come later and only if you switch them on.

Brain + heart

How a person decides

Logic and feeling are two layers. The brain scores what makes sense. The heart pushes those scores with urges nobody designed. A little structured randomness picks the winner, and every decision keeps its true reason.

score(option) = Π question curves (each 0–1) × heart urges (spite, longing, fear, pride, restlessness, devotion) × mood momentum choice = best score, or a near-best one for impulsive people

The heart

A tiny network per person, about 200 numbers: 16 inputs (mood, needs, memories, traits, who is near) and 8 urges. It is never trained. A child's heart blends their parents' plus mutation, and strong experiences nudge it (betrayal makes them suspicious for life). Families grow temperaments nobody designed.

Emotions with momentum

Anger fades over days, grief over a year, joy fades fast. Mood colours every score, so people act differently on a bad week.

Biases and impulses

Grudges outweigh facts. People trust kin over strangers, and pride and superstition get in the way. Rarely, someone acts on pure impulse: runs away, confesses at the festival, gambles the harvest.

Beliefs, not truth

People act on what they believe, rumours included. Misunderstandings start feuds, and the truth can surface decades later.

Life goals

"Become Lu Wen's disciple" breaks down into steps: raise Martial, earn his regard, pass the test. Ambitions span decades and are checked monthly or when something happens.

No language model

No AI writing thoughts or dialogue. The chronicle and life stories are written from templates driven by the real recorded reasons.

The rule

Simulate deeply, draw little

Thinking about a person is cheap: a few numbers once a day. Drawing a person, walking them and pathfinding is expensive. So almost everyone is simulated on paper, and only the people near the camera are real walkers.

TierWhoUpdatedCost
A walkers≤ 100 near the cameraevery frame: moving, pathing, drawnhighest, capped

Techniques
  • Packed arrays, not objects: one array per field across all people, which is fast to loop and small in memory.
  • Sparse storage: a person stores only the skills they touched (~40) and the ties they actually have (~40, about Dunbar's number).
  • Derived, not stored: kin, seniority and lineage are computed from parent and master links when needed.
  • Event-driven: opinion changes on events and a weekly drift, not every pair every day.
  • Time-sliced: the daily batch gets a fixed budget per frame (e.g. 2 ms), so the screen never stutters.
  • Coarser ticks when skipping: at "skip a century", ticks become weekly or monthly with scaled results.
  • Escape hatch: if GDScript is too slow at 100k, the daily batch moves to C# or a native module, and nothing else changes.
